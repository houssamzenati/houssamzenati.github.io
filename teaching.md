When teaching, I like to emphasize conceptual clarity, mathematical rigor, and active engagement, with the goal of helping students understand both the structure of a method and the assumptions that make it work.

## Courses and guest lectures

- **Introduction to Causal Inference with Hidden Confounders: Instruments and Proxies**, UM6P, 12 November 2025.<br>
  **Guest lecture.** A 90-minute lecture followed by a separate 90-minute practical, progressing from potential outcomes and adjustment with observed covariates to instruments, proxy variables, and bridge functions for hidden confounding. [[Slides]]({{ "/assets/slides/causal-inference-hidden-confounders-um6p-2025.pdf" | relative_url }})

- **Causal Inference**, New York University Paris, 2024--2025.<br>
  **Co-instructor**, with [Judith Abecassis](https://judithabk6.github.io/). This [course](https://www.nyu.edu/paris/academics/courant-semester-in-paris.html) introduces the main tools of causal inference, from randomized experiments to observational and quasi-experimental methods, with a particular emphasis on observational inference.

- **Advanced Machine Learning**, CentraleSupélec, 2024--2025.<br>
  **Teaching assistant**, alongside [Emilie Chouzenoux](https://pages.saclay.inria.fr/emilie.chouzenoux/). This [course](https://pages.saclay.inria.fr/emilie.chouzenoux/ECPml/index.htm) covers modern machine learning methods at the interface of statistics and optimization, with an emphasis on connecting theory, assumptions, and algorithmic practice.

- **Data Visualisation**, Université Paris Dauphine -- PSL, 2021--2022.<br>
  **Instructor** for Master's students in the IASD program, presenting data visualisation as a statistical and algorithmic tool for exploring, summarizing, and communicating complex data.
