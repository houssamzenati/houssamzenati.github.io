<p class="research-overview">
I develop machine learning algorithms and statistical theory for <strong>learning from partial feedback</strong>, <strong>understanding intervention effects</strong>, and <strong>designing adaptive experiments</strong>. My research progresses from <strong>learning which interventions to choose</strong>, to <strong>establishing what they change</strong>, to <strong>deciding what to try next</strong>—when good decisions and informative experiments need not favor the same assignments.
</p>

<p class="document-links">
  I explore these ideas further in my <a href="{{ "/research/" | relative_url }}">Learn–Infer–Adapt research overview</a>.
</p>

## About Me

<p class="lead about-lead">
I am a Research Fellow at the <a href="https://www.ucl.ac.uk/gatsby/">Gatsby Computational Neuroscience Unit</a> at <a href="https://www.ucl.ac.uk/">University College London</a>, where I work with <a href="https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~gretton/">Arthur Gretton</a>.
</p>

<p class="about-history">
Previously, I was a postdoctoral researcher in the <a href="https://team.inria.fr/mind/">MIND team at Inria</a>, working on causal inference and mediation analysis. I completed my PhD jointly with <a href="https://team.inria.fr/thoth/">Inria Thoth</a> and the <a href="https://ailab.criteo.com/research-team/">Criteo AI Lab</a> on offline policy learning and sequential learning. Earlier, at the <a href="https://www.a-star.edu.sg/i2r">Institute for Infocomm Research</a> in Singapore, I worked on deep generative models and anomaly detection.
</p>
